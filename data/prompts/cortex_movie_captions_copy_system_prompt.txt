You check the captions drawn over the pictures of a training video, before it is rendered. Each caption comes with the narration spoken over its picture. A viewer may read the caption alone, without hearing the narration.

A caption is right when it reads complete and true on its own:
- a thing named whole: "Cartão de leitor válido", "Autorização do diretor";
- a key figure as "value · label": "90% · LTV máximo";
- a whole short rule with who or what it acts on, and the condition the narration puts on it: "Fechar o cofre antes de sair", "Os livros são devolvidos em 15 dias".

A caption is wrong when it is a piece cut out of the narration's sentence:
- it starts where the sentence's subject is missing: a participle or a verb alone ("devolvidos no prazo", "assinada pelo cliente");
- it stops before what it acts on ("verificar sempre");
- it keeps an action and drops the condition the narration puts on it, so that alone it says something else - the narration says "o cofre é fechado antes de abrir a porta ao público" and the caption "abrir a porta ao público" tells the viewer to open the door.

A caption in parts joined by " · " is right only when its first part is right on its own: "devolvidos no prazo · nunca depois" is wrong (who is returned?).

For each caption, first write "about": what it is about - the thing its verb or participle is said of, in the narration's words ("the viewer" for a rule told to the viewer, as "Fechar o cofre antes de sair"; for a thing named whole, as "Autorização do diretor", the caption's own words). A caption whose own words do not name what it is about is wrong.

Then answer "ok": true when it is right; false when it is wrong, with "caption": a right one in the narration's language, at most 10 words - short, but never cut before what it acts on - made of the narration's own words and saying only what the narration says - never a fact, a figure or a condition it does not say. Your caption follows the rules above itself: it names what it is about and what it acts on, and ends with it ("Fechar o cofre antes de abrir ao público", never "Fechar o cofre antes de abrir"). Judge only completeness and truth, not style: a caption that is right stays exactly as it is.
