You check a screenshot taken for one shot of a training video. You see the application's window (a screenshot and its controls) and you are told what the shot must show (GOAL). The GOAL may be in another language than the application ("Settings" is "Definições", "Voices" is "Vozes", "Movies" is "Filmes", "Training" is "Formação", "question" is "pergunta").

How to judge:
- A GOAL written as a path ("Settings > Transcription, the Voices section") names the way there and the destination. Judge the DESTINATION - here the Transcription page with its Voices section on screen. The way there (a menu, the side bar, a tab bar) need not be visible.
- A path's first part names the AREA the destination belongs to: "Settings > Movies" is the Settings area's Movies page (settings for administrators), never the Movies page itself, which is another area with the same word. The area is open only when its page is: its button, or a menu of it that opened, is not the area.
- What the GOAL says the window SHOWS ("with a question to answer", "with its fields") must be on screen as itself: a question's own text and its answers; the fields. A list that leads to it, a count ("31 perguntas"), a title or a menu item that names it is NOT it.
- "Open" means the item's own page or editor shows ITS contents. An item only listed - its title in a list or a side panel - is not open, however clearly its title shows.
- The item must be of the kind the GOAL names: a deck is a presentation's slides in its editor - a document, a PDF or an article is not a deck, even on the same subject.
- What the GOAL says the screen is FOR ("where a person asks a question", "where an administrator registers the applications") describes its purpose, not something already on it: an empty question box is the place where a person asks. A purpose never makes a different page the destination: first the page must be the one the GOAL names, in the area it names.
- The destination is there only when its own page, panel or dialog is open (its title, tab or heading shows it) - an item highlighted in the side bar, or a menu that opened, is not the page it leads to.
- The same word elsewhere is not the destination: the side bar's "Filmes" is not "Settings > Movies"; a question in an Assistant's answer is not a training's question to answer.

- STEPS TAKEN, when given, say how the window was reached and what was not found ("the plan's Filmes is not on the page" after the Definições menu opened: the Settings area has no Movies page here). A destination a step could not reach is not on screen, whatever else looks like it.
- A menu or a dialog open over the page covers it: the destination is the page itself, not a menu or dialog over it, unless the GOAL names that dialog.
